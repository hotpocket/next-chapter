This part is the audio engine: the scripts that turn written sections into spoken chapters. The first, build_audio.py, clones a voice from one reference recording using Chatterbox, the text-to-speech engine, on a graphics card. It splits each section into chunks of up to three hundred characters on sentence boundaries, then drops any chunk with no word characters, a speakability filter that stops the model reading back its own voice sample's transcript. Every chunk of audio is cached under a content-addressed name, a hash of the chunk's text, the voice, and the synthesis parameters, so an edited sentence re-renders alone while every unchanged chunk stays a cache hit; the old index-keyed names forced a whole-book re-render after any edit, and chunk_cache.py migrates a legacy cache into content addressing and sweeps orphans. Chapters assemble into a chaptered book file, and a second script, build_m4a.py, produces the format the player consumes: one smaller file per chapter plus a manifest whose version is a content hash, so any audio change busts the player's cache.

Most of this engine predates the project's prompt exporting, so its provenance is commit messages and one recap — paraphrase and inference, honestly labeled. The exception is the Full and Summary track split from the July twenty-first session: a verbatim worry that the reviewer won't spend the time, gated on a sample summary, then a verbatim instruction to add the summary as the final step of generating a chapter — exactly where that pass sits in the code.
