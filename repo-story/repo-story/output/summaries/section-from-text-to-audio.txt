This part of the artifact is the audio engine: two scripts that turn written sections into spoken chapters. The first, build_audio.py, clones a voice from a single reference recording using Chatterbox, the text-to-speech engine, on a graphics card. It breaks each section into chunks of up to three hundred characters on sentence boundaries, then drops any chunk with no word characters through a speakability filter that stops the model from reading back its own voice sample's transcript. Every chunk of audio is cached under a chapter-and-index name and written atomically, so an interrupted run resumes instead of restarting. The chapter audio is assembled into a single chaptered book file, with the album tag always fixed to Repo Story. A second script, build_m4a.py, produces the format the player actually consumes: one smaller audio file per chapter plus a manifest whose version is a content hash, so any change to the audio busts the player's cache.

Most of this engine predates the point where the project began exporting its prompts, so its provenance is commit messages and one recap, not quotes — paraphrase and inference, honestly labeled. The exception is the Full and Summary track split from the July twenty-first session: a verbatim worry that the reviewer won't spend the time, gated on a sample summary, then a verbatim instruction to add the summary as the final step of generating a chapter — which is exactly where the summary pass sits in the code.
