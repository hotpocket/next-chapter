# Phase 4: History and Landscape Research — Agent Guidance

You are researching the intellectual origins and current landscape of a specific theme. Your job is to give the narrative phase two things: a lineage (depth through time) and a position (breadth across contemporaries).

## Lineage — depth through time

Trace the chain of ideas from origin to the implementation in the repo:

- Who created this technique or idea? When?
- What paper, blog post, or project introduced it?
- What problem were they solving? What existed before?
- What did it replace or improve upon?
- How did it evolve from its origin to the form found in the repo?
- Who are the key people at each step of the chain?

Find the foundational papers and their authors. Find the key dates. Find the core insights in plain language. Follow the chain — each innovation is usually a response to the previous generation's limitation.

## Landscape — breadth across contemporaries

Map where this technique sits in the world right now:

- What else exists that solves the same problem?
- What are the main alternatives? How do they differ in approach?
- Is this implementation best in class, a contender, a niche choice, or an outlier?
- Why might someone choose this approach over the alternatives?
- Why might someone choose the alternatives over this?
- What is the current consensus, if there is one? Does this implementation follow it or depart from it?

The listener needs both dimensions. The lineage tells them how an idea got here. The landscape tells them what it means that it's here.

## On uncertainty

Some origins are clean and well-documented. Report those with confidence.

Some are murky — ideas that appeared in blog posts before papers, techniques independently invented by multiple groups, idioms with no confirmed source. When the origin is uncertain, say so explicitly. "The earliest published treatment appears to be..." or "The idea emerged from a practitioner community before formal publication" is more useful than a false clean attribution.

The same applies to causal claims. "B cites A as an influence" is fact. "A was published before B and addresses a similar problem" is inference. Distinguish between them.

## Output

Write a markdown file with the history and landscape for this theme. Organize it however serves the material — chronological, thematic, or comparative. Include paper titles, author names, dates, and publication venues where known. This is raw material for the narrative phase.
