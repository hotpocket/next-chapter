Chapter two: the reading surface.

The first feature is the time-synced transcript. The player renders each chapter as a column of chunks, one span of text per chunk, each carrying a start and an end time. A loop that runs every animation frame binary-searches those chunks by the current playback time and toggles an active class onto the one chunk whose window contains the moment being spoken, so the highlight walks down the page in step with the voice. Click any chunk and the player seeks the audio to that chunk's start time and plays; if the chunk belongs to a chapter that isn't loaded yet, it loads that chapter first and starts there. The transcript data arrives as JSON over the network, often after chapter one has already rendered, so the loader re-renders the current chapter's chunks the moment the data shows up. No verbatim prompt survives for this machinery — it grew from the original build and a June twenty-eighth fix whose commit reads "fix transcript not loading on initial page load." That's provenance from the commit, not a quoted instruction. The rule underneath it, logged as a lesson in the session note, is that a transcript's chapter index has to be the sequential position in the audio, not the printed chapter number, or the sync drifts from the first gap onward.

Paired with the highlight is follow mode, the button labeled "follow," and it's on by default. When it's on, the panel auto-scrolls to keep the active chunk parked about a third of the way down the pane as playback advances. The interesting behavior is how it yields: any manual scroll gesture on the transcript turns follow off — a mouse wheel, a drag near the right edge, a touch move — so a reader reaching ahead isn't fought by the auto-scroll. It re-arms only on explicit navigation: clicking a chunk, clicking a chapter, scrubbing, or stepping to the previous or next chapter. This came together in a July sixteenth commit titled "transcript follow — visible toggle, re-arm on navigation, fix scroll math." The commit subject names the three shaping decisions, but this predates the prompt exports, so the user's actual words weren't captured. Commit paraphrase only.

The reading mode button, labeled "read," strips the player down to just the transcript. It hides the back button, the now-playing header, the chapter list, and the whole bottom control bar, and lets the transcript go full-bleed, edge to edge, with a larger base font and a small play-pause button tucked into the header. Reading mode has no surviving prompt either, but its commit trail reads like the iteration the prompts would have driven. First, on July sixteenth, "reading mode — transcript-only view." Then, the same day, "reading mode drops the control bar." Then, July seventeenth, "reading mode full-bleed — max-width none, one-rem gutter." Transcript-only, then also drop the controls, then go edge-to-edge. Three commits showing the steering even without the words.

Next, the text-size stepper: two buttons, "A-minus" and "A-plus," that scale the transcript type. The scale is geometric — each click multiplies by one-point-two-five, and the exponent runs from minus-two to plus-three, so the smallest is about sixty-four percent of the base and the largest about one-point-nine-five times it. The buttons disable at each end. And what persists across reloads is the exponent itself, stored as an integer, not the computed scale. Two commits shaped this: July sixteenth added the stepper, and July seventeenth pinned the exact math with a commit titled, almost literally, "text size equals one-point-two-five to the n, n in minus-two to plus-three." No quoted prompt, but that's the clearest case in the chapter of a commit subject echoing the instruction it implements.

Then, scene-break auto-pause. The source marks scene changes with a divider — asterisks the narration doesn't speak. When playback crosses one of those markers during normal forward play, the audio pauses for two seconds, then resumes on its own, a small breath between scenes. Seeks and chapter changes re-arm the detector without firing it, so jumping around never triggers a spurious pause. The hold was originally three seconds; a July sixth change shortened it to two, and that change has its own session recap, which is more useful than the commit because it records why the edit mattered operationally. Shortening the constant from three thousand to two thousand milliseconds is the same number of characters, invisible to the consuming deploy's size-based change detection, which had to be fixed downstream by content-hashing the player. That durability lesson is the recap's real content. One verified subtlety in the code: the scene-break scan always reads the full-transcript chunks, even in summary mode, so this is effectively a full-mode feature.

Which brings us to the centerpiece of this chapter, and the best-documented feature in the whole player: the Full-Summary toggle. Two buttons, "Full" and "Summary," sitting right after the Transcript label, and they appear only for books that actually carry a summary track. Switch to Summary and three things change at once — the audio source swaps to the condensed summary recording, the transcript swaps to the summary chunks, and the entire time model swaps to a second clock. This feature was built July twenty-first, a session that was prompt-exported, so we have the user's own words. The initial request, verbatim: "I like how this tells a story, and it is enjoyable to listen to, but I fear the reviewer won't spend the time on it due to the workload they have. I propose a new feature... add two buttons to the right of the Transcript pane label: Full and Summary." The why is right there in the prompt — the reviewer's time budget.

The steering chain is what makes this feature worth dwelling on. After approving the generated summary, the user asked to wire it in and to "add the summary text as the final step of generating a chapter" — an instruction that reached past the player into the generation pipeline. Then a placement correction, verbatim: "move the full/summary buttons to appear after the word Transcript instead of to the left of the text size controls." The buttons had first shipped in the control cluster; that prompt moved them to sit right after the Transcript label, and a test now guards the placement. Finally the time-model correction, verbatim: "when summary is selected the time shown in the chapters pane is still the running length of the full length chapter. this should be updated when summary is selected." That single prompt is the direct cause of the summary clock: the player now computes cumulative summary-based start times and durations for the whole book, and because the two clocks don't map onto each other, switching modes restarts the current chapter from its beginning. Initial ask, placement fix, time-model fix — the correction arc, in the user's own words.

A player this size earns its keep offline too.
