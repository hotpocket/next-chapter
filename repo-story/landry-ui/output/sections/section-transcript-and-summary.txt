Chapter two. This is the reading surface: the transcript panel and everything that acts on the text while the audio plays.

Start with the time-synced transcript. Every chapter renders as a column of chunks, and each chunk carries a start time and an end time. On every animation frame the player binary-searches those chunks against the current playback position and marks the one whose window contains it, so the highlight moves down the page with the voice. Chunks are also controls. Click one and the audio seeks to its start and plays; if the chunk belongs to a chapter that is not loaded, the player loads that chapter first and starts there. The transcript itself arrives as JSON over the network, and it often lands after chapter one has already rendered, so the loader re-renders the current chapter's chunks the moment the data arrives. No verbatim prompt survives for any of this. The provenance is a commit from June twenty-eighth, "fix transcript not loading on initial page load," plus a lesson recorded in the session notes: a transcript chapter's index must be its sequential position in the audio, not the printed chapter number, or the highlighting drifts from the first missing chapter onward.

Follow mode rides on that highlight. The button reads "follow," and it is on by default. While it is on, the panel auto-scrolls so the active chunk sits about a third of the way down the pane. What matters is how it gets out of the way: any manual scroll gesture on the transcript — a mouse wheel, a drag along the right edge, a touch move — switches follow off, so a reader skimming ahead is never fought by the auto-scroll. It comes back only on explicit navigation: clicking a chunk, clicking a chapter, scrubbing the track bar, or stepping between chapters. The shape of this comes from a July sixteenth commit, "transcript follow — visible toggle, re-arm on navigation, fix scroll math." That subject line names the three decisions, but the session predates the prompt exports, so the user's words are unrecorded — commit paraphrase only.

Next, reading mode. The button reads "read," and it strips the player to the transcript alone. The back button, the now-playing header, the chapter list, and the bottom control bar all disappear; the transcript goes full-bleed, edge to edge, with a larger base font and a small play-pause button tucked into the panel header. Again no prompt survives, but the commit trail records the iteration the prompts would have shown. July sixteenth: "reading mode — transcript-only view." Later the same day: "reading mode drops the control bar." July seventeenth: "reading mode full-bleed — max-width none, one-rem gutter." Transcript only, then lose the controls, then go edge to edge — three commits carrying the steering without the words.

The text-size stepper sits beside it: two buttons, A-minus and A-plus. The scale is geometric. Each press multiplies the type by one point two five, and the exponent is clamped between minus two and plus three, so the smallest setting is about sixty-four percent of the base and the largest is nearly double. The buttons disable at the ends of the range, and what persists across reloads is the exponent itself, stored as an integer, not the computed size. Two commits shaped it: July sixteenth introduced the stepper, and July seventeenth pinned the math with a subject that is nearly the instruction itself — "text size equals one point two five to the n, n in minus two to plus three." No quoted prompt, but no commit in this chapter echoes its instruction more literally.

Then scene-break auto-pause. The source text marks scene changes with asterisk dividers the narration never speaks. When playback crosses one during normal forward play, the audio pauses for two seconds and then resumes on its own — a breath between scenes. Seeks and chapter changes re-arm the detector without firing it, so jumping around never triggers a false pause. The hold began at three seconds; a July sixth session shortened it to two, and that change has its own recap note, which records something the commit cannot: changing the constant from three thousand to two thousand milliseconds leaves the file the same size, invisible to a consuming deploy that detected changes by file size, and that had to be fixed downstream by content-hashing the player. One verified subtlety in the code: the detector always scans the full transcript's chunks, even in summary mode, so in practice this is a full-mode feature.

Which brings us to the chapter's centerpiece and the best-documented feature in the book: the Full-Summary toggle. Two buttons, "Full" and "Summary," sit immediately after the Transcript label, shown only for books that actually carry summary tracks. Switching to Summary swaps three things at once: the audio source becomes the condensed summary recording, the transcript becomes the summary chunks, and the whole time model moves to a second clock built from summary durations. This was built on July twenty-first, in a prompt-exported session, so the user's own words survive. The initial request, verbatim: "I like how this tells a story, and it is enjoyable to listen to, but I fear the reviewer won't spend the time on it due to the workload they have. I propose a new feature." The prompt pointed at a screenshot of the transcript pane — the feature specified against what the user was looking at — and gave the instruction, verbatim: "add two buttons to the right of the Transcript pane label: Full and Summary." The motive is inside the prompt: the reviewer's time budget. A follow-up added a guardrail, also verbatim: "Don't do anything yet except generate the summary and show it to me."

Then the steering chain. The approval came with an instruction reaching beyond the player, verbatim: "the summary is good. incorporate the UI needed to flip between the summary and full text. add the summary text as the final step of generating a chapter." That last sentence pushed summary generation into the pipeline that builds the chapters; the player's job is to consume the summary track and summary chunks the pipeline emits. Next, a placement correction, verbatim: "move the full/summary buttons to appear after the word Transcript instead of to the left of the text size controls currently." The buttons had first shipped in the control cluster; that prompt moved them to sit right after the Transcript label, and a test now asserts the placement. Last, the time-model correction, verbatim: "when summary is selected the time shown in the chapters pane is still the running length of the full length chapter. this should be updated when summary is selected." That prompt is the direct cause of the summary clock. The player now computes cumulative summary-based start times and a summary-based total for the book, with chapters that lack a summary falling back to their full duration; and because the two clocks don't map onto each other, switching modes restarts the current chapter from its beginning, preserving whether it was playing. Initial ask, guardrail, approval, placement fix, time-model fix — a full correction arc in the user's own words.

A player this size earns its keep offline too.
